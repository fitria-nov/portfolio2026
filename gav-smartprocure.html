1.2 Background & Context

The aviation Maintenance, Repair, and Overhaul (MRO) industry operates under exceptionally stringent regulatory frameworks (such as DGCA and EASA). In this environment, the procurement of aircraft spare parts is not merely a supply chain function; it is a critical safety and operational imperative. A missing part can lead to an Aircraft On Ground (AOG) situation, costing airlines tens of thousands of dollars per hour in lost revenue and disrupted schedules.

Historically, MRO procurement processes have been heavily reliant on manual interventions, fragmented systems, and physical documentation. While SAP is often used as the core ERP, the peripheral processes—such as purchase request initiation, multi-tier approvals, and vendor communication—frequently occur via disjointed emails, spreadsheets, and paper forms. This fragmentation introduces significant latency, increases the risk of human error, and limits real-time visibility into inventory and budget utilization.

Digital transformation in this sector is critical. GAV SmartProcure is conceived as a bridge solution—a streamlined, automated interface that digitizes the end-to-end procurement lifecycle while seamlessly integrating with the core SAP backend. By eliminating manual touchpoints, the system aims to drastically reduce PR-to-PO cycle times, ensure robust compliance with BUMN (State-Owned Enterprise) regulations, and provide stakeholders with actionable, real-time insights.

SECTION 2: BUSINESS PROBLEM ANALYSIS

2.1 Industry Context Analysis

Procurement in the aviation MRO sector is distinctively challenging. Unlike retail or standard manufacturing, aviation parts are heavily regulated. Every part must possess proper certification (e.g., EASA Form 1, FAA 8130-3). The lead times for these specialized parts can be exceptionally long, and the cost of delay is astronomical (AOG scenarios). Therefore, a procurement system in this industry cannot simply be efficient; it must be flawlessly accurate, highly traceable, and exceptionally fast when emergencies arise.

2.2 Current State Assessment

Currently, the procurement process is highly fragmented. A mechanic identifies a need and fills out a paper or Excel-based Purchase Request. This form is physically routed to a manager, who may be off-site, causing immediate delays. Finance manually checks budget spreadsheets. Procurement staff re-type the request into SAP, leading to frequent data entry errors. When items arrive, the warehouse relies on manual checking against paper POs, delaying the update of inventory systems. This creates a reactive environment where staff spend more time chasing approvals and correcting errors than analyzing vendor performance or optimizing stock levels.

2.3 Root Cause Analysis (Ishikawa / Fishbone)

  • People:
    • Resistance to adopting complex SAP interfaces.
    • Lack of training on standardized material naming conventions.
    • Managers unavailable to sign physical documents.
  • Process:
    • Sequential, manual approval routing.
    • No standardized prioritization for emergency (AOG) vs. routine requests.
    • Manual budget verification step slows down the flow.
  • Technology:
    • Lack of mobile approval capabilities.
    • No real-time integration between front-end requests and SAP backend.
    • Data trapped in unstructured formats (Emails, PDFs).
  • Data / Information:
    • Duplicate material codes in the master database.
    • Inaccurate real-time inventory visibility.
  • Management:
    • Lack of visibility into process bottlenecks.
    • Performance metrics (SLAs) for procurement are not tracked.
  • Environment:
    • Fast-paced operational environment clashes with slow administrative processes.

2.4 5 Whys Analysis

Problem: Frequent delays in issuing Purchase Orders for critical parts. 1. Why? Because Purchase Requests (PRs) sit pending approval for an average of 4 days. 2. Why? Because the approval requires physical signatures or explicit email confirmations from multiple directors. 3. Why? Because there is no digital workflow system to automatically route and notify approvers based on value thresholds. 4. Why? Because the company relies on legacy manual SOPs developed before mobile technology was prevalent. 5. Why (Root Cause)? Because there has been a lack of investment in a bridge system that connects user-friendly digital workflows with the rigid core ERP.

2.8 Key Insights

  1. Speed vs. Control Dilemma: The business needs lightning-fast procurement for AOG situations, but strict compliance controls slow things down. Insight: The system must feature dynamic, rules-based routing that can expedite emergency requests while maintaining auditability.
  2. The "Last Mile" Problem: SAP is powerful but too complex for everyday users (mechanics, line managers). Insight: Building a user-friendly abstraction layer (SmartProcure) is more effective than trying to train all staff on raw SAP GUI.
  3. Data is the New Bottleneck: Manual data entry is the root of most errors and delays. Insight: Master data (materials, vendors, cost centers) must be tightly controlled and accessible via auto-complete/dropdowns to prevent garbage-in, garbage-out.

SECTION 4: AS-IS PROCESS FLOW

4.1 Process Overview

The current procurement process is highly manual, serial, and opaque. When a department needs a part, they fill out a physical or Excel PR form. This form is passed around for physical signatures. Once approved, it is handed to the Procurement department, where a staff member manually re-types the data into SAP. SAP generates a PO, which is exported to PDF and emailed to the vendor. When goods arrive, the warehouse manually checks the delivery against a printed PO and updates SAP, often with a delay.

4.2 Detailed AS-IS Process Steps

  1. Employee: Identifies need, manually fills out Excel PR form (looks up material codes manually). [Pain Point: High error rate in codes]
  2. Employee: Prints form, walks it to Manager's desk or sends via email.
  3. Manager: Reviews PR. If approved, signs physically or replies "Approved" via email. [Pain Point: Delay if manager is off-site]
  4. Employee: Forwards approved PR to Finance.
  5. Finance: Manually checks department budget spreadsheet. Signs if budget is available. [Pain Point: Time-consuming, manual check]
  6. Employee: Sends fully signed PR to Procurement Admin.
  7. Procurement Admin: Receives PR. Manually enters data into SAP GUI to create official SAP PR. [Pain Point: Double entry, risk of typos]
  8. Procurement Mgr: Reviews SAP PR, converts to SAP Purchase Order (PO).
  9. Procurement Admin: Exports PO to PDF, drafts email, attaches PDF, sends to Vendor.
  10. Vendor: Delivers goods to Warehouse with Delivery Note.
  11. Warehouse: Visually inspects goods, checks against printed PO.
  12. Warehouse: Manually logs receipt into SAP (Goods Receipt). [Pain Point: Often delayed to end of shift, causing inventory desync]
  13. Finance: Receives invoice, manually performs 3-way match (PO, GR, Invoice) before payment.

4.3 Process Metrics (Current State)

  • PR Creation to Final Approval: 3 - 5 Days.
  • Data Entry into SAP: 1 - 2 Days backlog.
  • Total PR-to-PO Cycle: 5 - 7 Days.
  • Error Rate (Material Codes/Quantities): ~12%.
  • Inventory Update Latency: 4 - 24 Hours after physical receipt.

4.4 Waste Analysis (Lean TIMWOODS)

  • Transport: Moving physical paper between desks.
  • Inventory: Buffer stock held because procurement is too slow to trust Just-In-Time.
  • Motion: Employees walking to get signatures.
  • Waiting: Waiting for managers to check email/return to desk to approve.
  • Over-processing: Re-typing data from Excel into SAP.
  • Defects: Incorrect material codes requiring PR rejection and rework.

SECTION 5: TO-BE PROCESS FLOW

5.1 Solution Design Principles

  1. Single Point of Entry: Data is entered once by the requestor and flows seamlessly.
  2. Automated Routing: The system decides who needs to approve based on business rules.
  3. Real-Time Validation: Budget and material codes are validated instantly upon input.
  4. Mobile-First Approvals: Managers can approve requests via smartphone with one click.
  5. Exception-Based Management: Procurement staff only intervene when the system flags an issue; routine POs are automated.

5.2 Detailed TO-BE Process Steps

  1. Employee: Logs into SmartProcure. Selects items from integrated Material Catalog (dropdowns/search). System auto-fills codes and prices.
  2. System: Instantly validates budget availability via API to SAP FI/CO. If insufficient, hard stop.
  3. Employee: Submits PR.
  4. System: Evaluates Approval Matrix and automatically routes to required approvers via push notification/email.
  5. Manager/Finance: Receives notification on mobile/web. Clicks "Approve". [Improvement: Instant, location-independent]
  6. System: Upon final approval, automatically syncs PR to SAP via API.
  7. System (Optional Auto-PO): If vendor is predefined and contract exists, system automatically converts PR to PO in SAP and emails Vendor. [Improvement: Zero manual data entry by Procurement]
  8. Vendor: Delivers goods.
  9. Warehouse: Scans PO barcode/QR code on delivery note using mobile device. Enters received quantity.
  10. System: Instantly syncs Goods Receipt to SAP, updating inventory in real-time. [Improvement: Zero latency, high accuracy]
  11. System: Triggers notification to Finance that 3-way match is ready for invoice processing.

5.3 Approval Matrix (Business Rules Engine)

The system will enforce the following dynamic routing rules: * Value < IDR 10,000,000: Direct Manager Approval only. * Value IDR 10,000,000 - 50,000,000: Manager + Finance Manager. * Value IDR 50,000,000 - 200,000,000: Manager + Finance Manager + VP Procurement. * Value > IDR 200,000,000: Manager + Finance Manager + VP Procurement + Board of Directors. * Emergency (AOG) Flag: Bypasses standard SLA, sends urgent SMS/Push alerts to all required approvers immediately.

  5. SAP returns { status: "OK", available: 150000000 }.
  6. Budget Service returns OK to PR Service.
  7. PR Service saves PR data to PostgreSQL Database.
  8. PR Service triggers Workflow Engine to start approval routing.
  9. Workflow Engine calls Notification Service.
  10. Notification Service sends Email/Push to Manager.
  11. PR Service returns 201 Created to Employee (UI).

SECTION 13: DATA FLOW DIAGRAM (DFD)

13.1 Context Diagram (Level 0)

The central process is the GAV SmartProcure System (0.0). * Employee sends Purchase Requests, receives Status Updates. * Manager/Approver receives Approval Requests, sends Approval Decisions. * Warehouse sends Goods Receipt Data, receives Open PO Details. * Vendor receives Purchase Orders, sends Delivery Notes / Invoices. * SAP ERP (External) receives Approved PRs/POs/GRs, sends Master Data, Budget Status, Inventory Levels.

13.2 Detailed DFD (Level 1)

  • Process 1.0 (Manage PR): Inputs material codes and quantities. Reads from D1: Material Master and D2: Budget. Writes to D3: Purchase Requests.
  • Process 2.0 (Process Approval): Reads from D3: Purchase Requests. Evaluates against D4: Approval Matrix. Writes status to D3 and creates logs in D5: Audit Log.
  • Process 3.0 (Manage PO): Reads Approved PRs from D3 and vendor data from D6: Vendor Master. Writes to D7: Purchase Orders.
  • Process 4.0 (Manage Inventory): Reads Open POs from D7. Takes GR input from Warehouse. Writes to D8: Inventory and updates D7 status.

SECTION 15: HIGH-LEVEL SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

15.1 Architecture Tiers

  1. Presentation Tier (Frontend): React.js single-page application (SPA). Fully responsive for mobile approvals. Uses a UI component library aligned with corporate branding.
  2. Application Tier (Backend APIs): Node.js/Express REST APIs following a microservices pattern (PR Service, Workflow Service, Integration Service).
  3. Integration Tier (Middleware): SAP PI/PO (Process Integration) or custom API Gateway to handle RFC/BAPI calls to the legacy SAP ERP safely.
  4. Data Tier:
    • PostgreSQL: Primary database for PR states, workflow history, and application-specific data.
    • SAP HANA: The ultimate system of record for master data (materials, vendors, ledger).

15.2 Security Architecture

  • Authentication: Integration with corporate Microsoft Active Directory via SAML 2.0 / OAuth 2.0 (SSO).
  • Authorization: Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) enforced at the API level (JWT tokens).
  • Data Protection: TLS 1.3 for data in transit. Database encryption for data at rest.

SECTION 17: VALIDATION & BUSINESS RULES

17.1 Hard Validation Rules

  • VR-01 (Budget): IF PR.TotalAmount > AvailableBudget(CostCenter) THEN Prevent_Submit AND Display_Error.
  • VR-02 (Delegation): A user cannot delegate approval authority to a user with a lower hierarchical rank.
  • VR-03 (Goods Receipt): IF GR.Quantity > (PO.OrderedQuantity - GR.PreviousReceived) THEN Flag_Variance AND Require_Manager_Override.

17.2 Segregation of Duties (SoD)

  • Rule 1: The Requestor_ID can never equal the Final_Approver_ID on the same PR.
  • Rule 2: The person recording the Goods Receipt (Warehouse) cannot be the person who created the PR.

SECTION 19: INTEGRATION FLOWS

19.1 SAP Integration Points

  1. Material Master Sync (Scheduled Batch): Pulls new/updated material codes from SAP to SmartProcure DB every 24 hours via API to ensure local dropdowns are fast and accurate.
  2. Real-time Budget Check (Synchronous API): Upon PR submission, SmartProcure pings SAP FI/CO. Must return result in < 2 seconds.
  3. PR/PO Posting (Asynchronous Queue): Once fully approved, the PR payload is placed in a message queue (e.g., RabbitMQ). A worker service consumes this and writes to SAP via BAPI to ensure no data loss if SAP is temporarily down.
